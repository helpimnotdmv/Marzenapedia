# Commandement des Forces Missiles

The **Commandement des Forces Missiles** (**CFM**) is the permanent functional command responsible for preparing and conducting designated strategic-strike missions of the [[Forces Armées Marzenières]] (FAM). It maintains a standing cadre for strategic planning, deterrence assessment and operational preparation, exercises permanent operational command over land-based missile formations, and receives designated naval or air contributions for approved missions.

CFM is a joint operational institution with strong historical and professional ties to the [[Armée de l'Air et de l'Espace]] (AAE). Its ground missile units belong to the AAE for service administration, personnel and technical support. Its joint headquarters reports through the **Chef d'état-major des armées** (CEMA). It stands separately from the joint enabling services.

The command combines an enduring deterrent responsibility with a mission-specific authority to direct strategic operations. In routine conditions, strike aircraft and naval platforms remain under their normal service and assigned joint arrangements. Following presidential approval of a designated mission, the CEMA can place particular aircraft detachments or naval platforms under CFM's direct operational command. **Approval of the mission and final authorisation to release weapons are separate presidential decisions.**

## Mission and doctrine

CFM maintains military options whose strategic significance warrants national reservation. Standing policy defines categories of strategic employment, while each operation receives an explicit presidential designation and mandate. Weapon range, aircraft type or the possession of a cruise missile does not by itself determine which command conducts a mission.

The command's doctrine gives priority to a **credible, selective conventional response**. National leaders should possess a means of answering a serious threat or undertaking a bounded strategic intervention without making a general campaign the only available military option. The ability to prepare and coordinate such a response contributes to deterrence before any decision to employ weapons.

Several delivery communities support that purpose. Land-based missiles, designated strike aircraft and suitable naval platforms offer different operational possibilities. Their combination gives national command more than one means of pursuing an approved objective and allows the contribution to be chosen according to the mission. CFM provides the enduring organisation through which those otherwise separate capabilities can be prepared for a common strategic task.

The preservation of a meaningful response after an initial attack forms a further part of the doctrine. Continuity of command and the endurance of the force are intended to deny an adversary confidence that an opening action would remove Marzena's choices. Public doctrine establishes this objective without publishing the force's readiness, locations or detailed arrangements.

Deterrence is treated as a continuing assessment problem. CFM contributes military analysis to work involving [[Direction du renseignement de la défense|DRD]], [[Direction du Renseignement Extérieur et Technique|DRET]] and the national policy authorities. The assessment considers the utility of available options, their credibility and the possible reactions to their preparation or employment. It does not presume that an opponent will interpret a military signal exactly as intended.

The command is conventionally armed. Its historical connection to the former nuclear programme belongs to the wider development of national strategic capability; it does not establish a contemporary nuclear mission.

## National authority and mission command

### Approval, preparation and release

The President authorises designated strategic missions and retains the subsequent decision on weapon release. By convention, those decisions follow deliberation in, or consultation with, the National Security Council. The Council's advisory role does not replace presidential decision or the military chain responsible for execution.

Presidential approval of a mission package allows the CEMA to assign the designated contribution to CFM. Within the approved mandate, CFM can direct its preparation and authorised posture, including deployment or presence activity where specified. Those actions may make a strategic option available without a later decision to use it.

Final launch authorisation is separate. The national military chain conveys the decision and the conditions governing its execution. CFM's commander exercises operational responsibility within those conditions and the decisions delegated by the mandate.

| Stage | Authority and effect |
| --- | --- |
| Routine planning | CFM develops strategic options with intelligence and service support; naval and air units remain under normal command arrangements |
| Presidential mission approval | Establishes the mandate; the CEMA assigns designated platforms or detachments to CFM |
| Authorised preparation | CFM commands the assigned contribution within the permitted posture and preparatory scope; weapon release remains reserved |
| Presidential release decision | Authorises execution under the specified conditions |
| Completion of the assignment | CFM reports completion; the CEMA formally releases temporarily assigned forces back to their normal arrangements |

The commander's discretion is defined for the mission. A mandate can delegate particular operational decisions while reserving others because of their political or military significance. The existence of a permanent strategic command does not make every contingency plan an authorised operation.

### Relationship with the CIO

CFM's relationship with the **Centre interarmées des opérations** (CIO) depends on the mandate. A standalone strategic intervention can use direct CEMA direction through CFM. A designated strategic mission within a wider campaign can place CFM within the CIO's campaign direction, while CFM retains direct command of its assigned strike contribution.

An activated coordination group connects the headquarters for the mission. It organises the relationship between strategic execution, the wider campaign and supporting forces. The presidential mandate and military assignment identify which decisions belong to each command.

The CFM commander normally retains personal operational responsibility for the strategic mission, supported by the permanent staff. Naval and aviation expertise is obtained through the relevant service headquarters and their specialists. Platform commanders retain their professional responsibilities within the assigned operational chain.

## Permanent organisation and assigned forces

### Headquarters and ground formations

CFM's principal headquarters is in Lévane. It maintains a compact command staff organised around strategic planning, current operational preparation and technical readiness work. The cadre is predominantly composed of career personnel with enduring experience in missile forces and strategic command.

The land-based force provides its permanent operational core. These formations remain under CFM in routine conditions as well as during a designated operation. Their AAE service affiliation supplies the personnel, maintenance and professional system within which they are sustained.

The current public outline groups the ground establishment into two principal formations:

| Formation | Principal responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| **Groupement des missiles stratégiques** | Land-based missile units assigned to the national strategic mission |
| **Groupement d'appui des forces missiles** | Dedicated technical support and preparation of the permanent ground establishment |

Subordinate operating units and their detailed equipment holdings are not enumerated in the public account. The broader infrastructure includes dispersed operating and support establishments. Their precise locations and current readiness remain classified.

The Army's rocket artillery and limited tactical ballistic-missile capability retain their ordinary Army and campaign responsibilities. They do not enter the permanent CFM establishment simply because their weapons are missiles. Relevant coordination takes place through the joint operational arrangements.

### Naval and air contributions

CFM prepares for naval and air contributions through a pool of qualified units. Regular joint certification establishes their suitability for the strategic role, while their services retain control of routine activity. The particular contribution is selected when a mission receives presidential approval.

For a strategic air mission, the approved package identifies the aircraft detachment assigned to CFM. Until that approval, strike aircraft remain under standard AAE and CIO arrangements for campaign and routine missions. The same aircraft community can consequently support ordinary campaign strike and a nationally reserved mission at different times without changing its permanent institutional home.

A designated naval platform, including a suitable cruise-missile submarine, can similarly pass under CFM's operational command for an approved assignment. Its crew, maintenance and permanent ownership remain Navy responsibilities. Strategic assignment does not create a separate CFM submarine fleet.

During transfer, the strategic mission takes priority and routine tasks are suspended. Essential responsibilities of the platform continue. Supporting aviation—including tanker or surveillance contributions—remains under the appropriate CIO and service command arrangements and provides coordinated support to CFM.

After the assignment is complete, CFM reports through the military chain and the CEMA formally releases the temporarily assigned contribution. It then returns to its normal service arrangements, including any continuing joint assignment. Permanent ground missile units remain under CFM operational command.

### Scale and accounting

The standing headquarters is described at the scale of several hundred personnel, while the permanent ground and dedicated support establishment comprises several thousand. These broad descriptions concern institutional scale rather than the number immediately available for an operation.

| Personnel category | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Joint headquarters and permanent cadre | Standing CFM establishment |
| Permanent ground missile and support formations | AAE service establishment under continuing CFM operational command |
| Temporarily assigned naval and air personnel | Remain on their parent service establishments; operational assignment does not add them again to national totals |

The size of an activated mission contribution varies. Neither its temporary assignment nor the existence of a qualified pool establishes a permanently available force of that size. Detailed readiness assessments are classified.

## Strategic preparation and technical support

CFM maintains long-range target packages through continuing work with the services and intelligence organisations. The contributing institutions supply relevant assessments and platform expertise; CFM integrates the military options and keeps the material prepared for national consideration. Maintaining a package does not constitute approval to execute it.

A permanent DRD liaison team connects the command to military intelligence. CFM draws on that support and on national assessment rather than duplicating DRD with a separate broad intelligence institution. DRET contributions reach the process through the appropriate national arrangements.

The strategic command network uses shared military infrastructure with a dedicated mission layer. Relevant planning, readiness reporting and supporting information connect to SICO, while reserved strategic functions use specialist systems and access arrangements.

Network testing is an enduring responsibility of the cadre. It includes command exercises and the operational acceptance of new or modified systems. These activities establish whether the institutions and equipment can fulfil the command's requirements; their technical procedures and results are not public.

CFM works with OCPM, the services and industry on the capabilities required for its mission. OCPM retains its wider technical and programme responsibilities. The detailed division of sensitive development work belongs to the respective programmes and is not disclosed through the command's public organisation.

## History

### Strategic planning origins

CFM developed from the national strategic-planning cadre associated with missile development and the preparation of reserved military options. Its strong AAE ties reflect the service's role in the personnel, technical and operational foundations of that work. The planning institution preceded the full development of its present cross-service command responsibility.

The **War of Sands, 1978–1982**, strengthened the argument for a national organisation able to connect strategic planning with usable military options. The subsequent reform placed greater emphasis on coordinated deterrence as a means of discouraging another wider war. During the 1980s, CFM acquired a broader cross-service operational role while retaining a permanent ground missile core.

The historical relationship with the nuclear programme continued through personnel and technical experience. Marzena's accession to the NPT in 1994 and the later retirement of its declared breakout infrastructure form the background to the present conventional posture. The programme's wider history is treated separately.

### A retained strategic responsibility

When the modern General Staff operational system developed, CFM's nationally reserved role was preserved. The expansion of CIO campaign direction therefore did not absorb every strategic mission into an undifferentiated campaign structure. CFM continued to provide a permanent institution for the distinct preparation and command requirements of designated strategic action.

The relationship became more consequential as Marzena developed additional air and maritime delivery options. Those capabilities remained in their services, while CFM maintained the means to direct selected contributions under a common strategic mandate.

### Acknowledged crisis activation

One crisis activation has been publicly acknowledged. It concerned the preparation of a strategic military option and its political significance, rather than an acknowledged strike. The episode did not result in weapon employment or wider escalation.

The relevant contingency, participating forces and detailed sequence remain classified. The acknowledgement establishes that the command was activated, but provides no basis for attributing the outcome to a particular platform or claiming that a specific adversary changed its policy because of it.

## Institutional character and contemporary role

CFM's professional culture emphasises continuity, precision and disciplined responsibility. Long-serving personnel preserve the knowledge required to maintain strategic options between crises and to exercise the assigned command when a mission is approved. Its public identity rests principally on the institution and its acknowledged responsibilities.

The recurring institutional tension concerns the relationship between strategic commitments and service availability. The Navy and AAE must sustain routine responsibilities while preparing qualified contributions to national options. Certification, planning and the assignment of a platform connect these obligations without making every capable unit permanently unavailable to its service.

Contemporary development emphasises the readiness of the existing force. The content of that work, programme priorities and internal readiness findings remain classified. CFM's public role is consequently clearer than the condition or composition of the force available on a particular date: it maintains the capacity to prepare a selective conventional response and, under the required national decisions, to command its execution.
